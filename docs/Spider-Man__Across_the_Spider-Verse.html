Plot

Miles Morales catapults across the Multiverse, where he encounters a team of Spider-People charged with protecting its very existence. When the heroes clash on how to handle a new threat, Miles must redefine what it means to be a hero.

Accolades

  • This film earned a nomination for Academy Award (Oscar) in 2023 in "Best Animated Feature" category.[25]
  • This film won American Film Institute Award in 2023 in "Top 10 Films" category.[26]
  • This film earned a nomination for Ray Bradbury Nebula Award for Outstanding Dramatic Presentation in 2024.[27]

Notes

  • Originally slated to be released on April 8, 2022, this film was later pushed back to October 7, 2022,[28] and then again to June 2, 2023.[29]
  • The film was originally announced as Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse - Part One,[30] though it later dropped the "Part One" from the title.[29] However, packaging for the Marvel Legends toy figures for the film's characters retains the original full title.

Trivia

  • Even before a script was finalized or a villain chosen, the production team had set on Across the Spider-Man being "a story about family, and the struggle of being both a son and a Spider-Man."[31]
  • A time-stamp in the security camera in Lenny's bodega places the events of the film set in Earth-1610B on 2023/07/11. This contradicts Miles' narration stating that he has been Spider-Man for one year and four months, since the events of Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse occurred in December 2018.
  • A set piece from Miles' first fight with the Spot that was cut from the film involved the two characters teleporting through a Chinese restaurant.[32]
  • In a concept-stage version of Miles' talk with Gwen during Jefferson's party, Rio saw them at a greenhouse rather than at a water tower.[33]
  • In a piece of concept art, Miles and Gwen visited Hobie's reality. The aesthetic of Earth-138 was inspired by 1970s punk posters, namely the use of overly processed/Xeroxed images, cutout shapes, and hand-done linework.[34]
  • A scene cut from the film featured Jessica Drew fighting a real cyborg octopus version of Doctor Octopus.[5]
  • Alex Ross' Spider-Man design for Sam Raimi's Spider-Man appears in this film in the form of one of the many alternate Spider-Men in the Spider Society.
  • The film features the return of the Spider-Men from several animated TV series, namely Spider-Man Unlimited, Spectacular Spider-Man and Ultimate Spider-Man. Spider-Man and His Amazing Friends and the 1994 Spider-Man animated TV series also have presence in the film in the form of Videoman making an appearance and Earth 65B's Peter Parker briefly wearing 1994 Peter's iconic polo shirt, respectively.
  • Multiple duplicates of the same Spider-Men appear in the film, Spider-Man Unlimited being one of the most prominent cases since four different versions of him appeared in different scenes and in different locations back to back.
  • The following Spider-Man variants were considered for the film but did not make it into the final cut were the Ultimate Spider-Woman, Ben Reilly as the Sensational Spider-Man, the Man-Spider, a S.H.I.E.L.D. Spider-Man, Supaidāman and his Leopardon,[35] the Zombie Spider-Man, the Night-Spider, Spider-Man 1602, Spider-Ma'am, Spider-Chef, the Amalgam Universe's Spider-Boy, a Turkish Spider-Man from the unlicensed film 3 Dev Adam, the Dormammuverse Spider-Man, Ashley Barton, a robot Spider-Man, a Spider-Wizard, a fox mascot for the Samurai Spider-Man,[36][37] and a Toy Biz Spider-Man being held by a giant child's hand off-screen.[38]
  • Some villains that were also considered for the film but also did not make it into the final cut were the Stilt-Man, the Trapster, the Wall, Agent Venom, Electro, the White Rabbit, Jack O'Lantern, the Scorpion (Earth-1610B), the Ultimate Mysterio, a cavewoman Boomerang, a TV-head Screwball, a few different variants of the Green Goblin (a Ghost Rider Green Goblin, a skier one, a figurine one and a puppet one), the Ox, the Human Fly, the Hippo, Xandu, 8-Ball, Chtylok, Hammer Harrison, the Radioactive Man, and the fully original character of Coffee Man. Most of the villains were designed for a scene taking place in the Bar with No Name that was cut from the film.[37] Tiger Shark, the Clam, and a variant of Diamondhead as prisoners of the Spider Society and another variant of Diamondhead in Earth-138B ultimately made it into the final film. The cut scene of the Bar with No Name also featured its proprietor, Delilah, who makes an appearance in Marvel's Spider-Man 2.
  • Some art styles explored for other realities in the film include Mike Mignola / Hellboy,[39][40] a Jack Kirby-inspired universe featuring Leopardon,[35] Joseph Christian Leyendecker,[41] a N. C. Wyeth-inspired reality for the Web-Slinger,[42] Provensens,[43] Zap Comix,[44] Monument Valley,[45] Moebius,[46] Ashley Wood[47] and a Cartoon Modern-styled reality.[48]
  • Early in the film's development, different styles and characters were explored. Among these were the Sinister Six from Norse mythology and a Kraven in mid-18th century miniature painting-style.[49]
  • The "canon events" listed are each references to various different Spider-Man stories, most of which happened to the mainstream Earth-616 variant of the character, although many suspiciously did not occur, or have not occurred, in the history of most Spider-Totems. These canon events include the following:
    • The death of the Spider-Totem's original love interest. This one is unconfirmed, but is alluded to by Gwen when she states that every time Spider-Man and Gwen Stacy fall in love, it does not end well.
    • The Spider-Totem's origin of getting spider-powers from being bitten by a radioactive spider.
    • The Spider-Totem receiving an upside-down kiss.
    • The Spider-Totem bonding with the Venom symbiote.
    • The death of an inspirational figure that teaches the Spider-Totem a variation of the saying "with great power comes great responsibility". This tends to be Ben Parker, but for Gwen it is Peter Parker and for Miles it is Aaron Davis.
    • The death of a police captain close to the Spider-Totem (typically George Stacy) from falling rubble. This event is classified "ASM90", a reference to the comic book issue that this took place in within the universe of Earth-616.
      • Although Miguel states that the police captain is crushed by rubble trying to save a child, one of the captains shown is George Stacy's Earth-120703 variant, who was stabbed by the Lizard instead of being crushed by rubble, and was simply trying to fight the Lizard as opposed to saving a child. Arguably, the child was his universe's Peter Parker.
    • The Spider-Totem temporarily quitting being a super-hero.
    • The Spider-Totem pushing themselves out of rubble that has fallen on top of them.
    • The Spider-Totem getting married.
    • The Spider-Totem crawling out of their own grave.
  • The film features a cameo from Metro Boomin, who executive-produced the soundtrack to the film, as a version of Spider-Man.
  • According to Phil Lord and Chris Miller, Margo Kess/Spider-Byte took the longest time of the characters to design and animate due to the animators intensely studying late 1980s to mid-1990s hologram technology for her movements and overall appearance.
  • It is implied that the records of the Spider Society, in addition to the members themselves, feature various variants of established characters in addition to the established characters themselves. For example, in the records of the canon event detailing the upside-down kiss, Peter Parker-96283 is seen with his upside-down kisses with both Mary Jane Watson-96823 and Gwen Stacy-96283. It can be implied that a variant of this Peter ended up in a relationship with Gwen in contrast to the Earth-96283 variant, who is in a relationship with Mary Jane. This could likely explain why some versions of characters, like Spider-Man Unlimited, occasionally have multiple versions of themselves on-screen at the same time.
