Teresia Karisik,[1] the woman simply known as Sage, is a mutant whose brain functions operate similarly to a computer, granting her perfect memory and rapid analytical abilities.[8] A survivor from a village torn by war, she found the telepath Charles Xavier by chance in the Hindu Kush mountain range right after he had his legs crushed by an attack.[9] Xavier mentored her in the use of her abilities to shape her into a spy, forging the identity of "Tessa" and ordering her to infiltrate the nefarious Hellfire Club as Sebastian Shaw's secretary and counselor.[10]

For years, "Tessa" worked as Shaw's loyal right hand.[11] Having become a target for the mutant predator Elias Bogan, she was rescued by one of Xavier's students, the X-Man Storm[12] and offered her services to the X-Men.[13] Still holding grudge against Xavier for having submitted her to a depreciative mission, she joined Storm's splinter group, the X-Treme X-Men, as Sage.[14] She ultimately left the X-Men to stage a coup and seize influence over the Hellfire Club,[15] which occasionally led her to join the British group known as Excalibur[16] and then the multiversal Exiles.[17]

After returning home,[18] Sage has been involved in functionalities of the mutant nation of Krakoa, being in charge of monitoring the transit of citizens through gateways.[19] As such, she operated as an intelligence agent for X-Force and was deeply entrenched in matters regarding national security.[20] Following the fall of Krakoa, Sage has been recruited by the mutant inventor Forge as a valuable asset of his own version of X-Force.[6]

History

Origins[]

As a young girl, Teresia Karisik[1] struggled by herself to survive in a war-torn Balkan village. Although she was able to put the threat of landmines and snipers from her mind at times, enemy troops eventually invaded her home and she was forced to flee the only life she had known and to leave no relationships behind.[21][22][23] She was unable to escape war, being forged by it in becoming a teenage loner rebel. During this period, her mutant abilities manifested, though their nature were a mystery to her. Gifted with a computer-like mind, she could store perfect recollections of her experiences and analyze available information in order to achieve precise conclusions.[9][11]

Caught up in the Russian invasion of Afghanistan, the peculiar teenage girl had to survive encounters with both soldiers from Kabul and local bandits by scavenging weapons and equipment from combatants who had fallen. One day, amidst a battle, she was contacted by a presence, being compelled to enter a cave in the Hindu Kush mountain range. There, she was met by the powerful telepathic mutant Charles Xavier, who had just had his legs crushed by an encounter with a "demon". Being wary of him at first, the girl learned about her unique condition as a mutant and chose to save the injured man. While freeing Xavier, she found a Shaw Industries / United Nations aid convoy being attacked by terrorists. Although she initially planned to evade them, she picked off the attackers from her hillside vantage point after noticing their cruel methods. Standing horrified over the body of a brutalized young aid worker, she chose no mercy for the attackers and eliminated them, despite their pleas for mercy.[9]

Xavier survived the incident partially thanks to the mutant girl's intervention. In return, he brought her to America, right when he had begun forming the first iteration of his team of teenage mutants, the X-Men. While the X-Men operated in public as heroes, the deadly girl had her presence kept as a secret. Instead, Xavier trained her as his personal spy. Her mission was to infiltrate the influential organization formed by the rich and the powerful known as the Hellfire Club. Renamed as "Tessa", she would use her facetious skills to pretend to be Sebastian Shaw's trusted secretary and counselor. A mutant himself, Shaw planned to elevate his positions inside the Hellfire Club's Inner Circle, and Xavier would have access to privileged information about his actions through Tessa.[10][9]

Lady Tessa of the Hellfire Club[]

Shaw had gathered other mutants around himself inside the Hellfire Club, including Tessa, his lover Lourdes Chantel, the formidable telepath Emma Frost, and the vigorous Harry Leland. In order to make his way to the Hellfire Club's higher ranks, Shaw cooperated with the president of the club, Edward Buckman, supporting Project: Armageddon to apparently commercialize mutant powers. In her analysis, Tessa suggested Shaw to regard Buckman as an enemy. Her predictions proved to be true when Frost learned that Buckman worked in coalition with Stephen Lang to eradicate the mutant race by weaponizing Sentinels. As a response, a Sentinel attacked one of Shaw's properties, which culminated with Chantel's sacrifice to save Shaw and her allies. In response, Shaw assassinated Buckman with Frost's help, and they ascended as the Lord Cardinals of the Hellfire Club. Tessa observed the coup and became the personal assistant of the new president of the Hellfire Club, the Black King Sebastian Shaw.[11]

As Lords Cardinal, Shaw and Frost ran afoul one of the Hellfire Club's most valued patrons, the reclusive billionaire and psychic predator Elias Bogan. Shaw proposed a wager with Bogan's minions, Oliver Ryland. If Shaw won, his fortune would be made, whereupon if he lost, Frost would belong to Bogan. The confident Bogan did not anticipate Tessa's analytical skills would certainly guarantee Shaw's improbable victory. Even though Bogan honored the outcome, he decided to get revenge on Tessa for the outcome, since he could not tolerate the feeling of being tricked or of losing.[12]

Under new mutant management, the Inner Circle of the Hellfire Club made their way into direct conflict with the X-Men as the illusionist Jason Wyngarde brought the X-Man Jean Grey under his influence and presented her as a potential Black Queen to assure his own bid for membership.[7] While Tessa was in no position to defend one of Xavier's students, she would still regret her inaction.[24] The rise of Sebastian Shaw also caused British S.T.R.I.K.E. secret agent, Betsy Braddock, to be assigned to infiltrate the Hellfire Club. Tessa, seeing her as brave and innocent as Jean Grey, confronted her, reflecting a psychic attack, thus convincing her to abandon her mission as a way not to have Braddock corrupted.[24]

During an arms deal between the Hellfire Club and Peter Coelho, Tessa spotted Ms. Marvel spying on the deal, which was enough to startle her into revealing herself. Ms. Marvel was defeated by the Hellfire Bishops Harry Leland and Donald Pierce, being forced to flee. Ms. Marvel then boarded a flight to Hong Kong to seduce Coelho for information. In the following days, Tessa secretly watched Ms. Marvel's interacting with Coelho to gather data. After Tessa informed Shaw that Coelho was reselling the armaments to Mystique's Brotherhood, he died at Shaw's hands.[25]

Ally of X[]

As the mutant-hating member of the Inner Circle, Pierce ultimately showed his true colors when staging a coup to compromise Shaw's position. He kidnapped Tessa, keeping Shaw under-resourced. As part of his plan, Pierce targeted young mutants and made Xavier his second hostage. Xavier's newest proteges, the New Mutants, defeated Pierce to free Tessa and Xavier. Upon her return to the Hellfire Club, Pierce was brought into custody at the Shaw Industries.[26] Soon after, Wyngarde went rogue as well and psychically attacked Emma Frost. Tessa took care of a harmed Frost when she looked for help.[27]

A new contender for the Inner Circle, Selene, offered Shaw an entranced Rachel Summers and Magma as slaves in order to prove her power. Tessa instantly recognized in Rachel a resemblance to Jean Grey. Before Tessa could advise Shaw to accept the deal as a way to use the powerful mutant girls to overpower Selene, Rachel and Magma broke free and were rescued by Xavier and the X-Men.[28] Despite this setback, Selene became the Black Queen of the Hellfire Club regardless,[29] forcing Tessa to mediate between Shaw's and Selene's egos. With Tessa's support, Selene convinced the Inner Circle to hunt down Rachel, who had tapped into the Phoenix powers. The X-Men did the same and their hunts were interrupted by Nimrod.[30] As a result, the Inner Circle was left heavily injured. Threatened with the arrival of the police, Tessa offered sanctuary to the X-Men at the Hellfire Club after calling for a truce.[31]

Gradually, the X-Men got closer to the Hellfire Club and their members Magneto and Storm jointly joined the Hellfire Club as its White King.[32] As the headmaster of Xavier's School, Magneto brought the New Mutants to a Hellfire Gala. Seeing an opportunity to gather intel, Karma stole away from the party and into the Club's depths. She attempted to psychically possess Tessa, succeeding only through surprise. Tessa was commanded to use the Hellfire Club's systems to search for Karma's missing siblings. Being unsuccessful, Karma was caught by Magneto and Shaw. Tessa freed herself unaffected by the possession and asked for the Hellfire Kings to be compassionate about the situation, revealing she had let Karma subdue her on purpose. Additionally, she revealed that she had already been searching for Karma's siblings at Magneto's request with no success. Devastated, Karma asked Tessa to deliver a farewell letter to Mirage as she left the New Mutants.[33]

Tessa investigated Pierce's escape from Shaw Industries with a cadre of Hellfire Knights and concluded that he was not a matter of interest to the Hellfire Club any longer.[34] Soon after, she accompanied Shaw as he met with Senator Robert Kelly at the Hellfire Club. Kelly's wife, Sharon, a former employee of Hellfire, perished in an attack carried out by the Master Mold against the X-Men after the meeting.[35] Tessa analyzed the situation for Shaw and foreseeable predicament was expected to be a harsher stance against mutants by Kelly's part in the government.[36]

Death of Shaw[]

At some point, Shaw was murdered at the hands of his son, Shinobi.[37] As the membership passed on to Shinobi, Tessa served him in his father's place. During Betsy Braddock's and Warren Worthington III's visit to the Hellfire Club during a Gala, Tessa psychically knocked out them as per Shinobi's request, allowing Worthington to be offered the status of White King in private, which he refused.[38]

When Benedict Kine challenged Shinobi Shaw to kill John Jonah Jameson over an expose on the Hellfire Club, Tessa summoned Hellfire Knights to perform the action, either murder or save Jameson. Secretly, she sent an anonymous telepathic call to the X-Men, forcing them to interfere with the plot by teaming up with Spider-Man. In the end, Tessa's plan was revealed by Jean Grey. Tessa shared her intentions, claiming that for the Hellfire Club's best interests no battle should have been carried out.[39]

With the return of Sebastian Shaw, who had actually survived the murder attempt, Tessa rejoined him. Together, they provided an exosuit to Holocaust at Shaw Industries in order to keep his powers in check. Shaw relied on Tessa's to keep the violently unpredictable Holocaust in check. In return, Shaw had Holocaust help him capture X-Force.[40] Tessa mentally conditioned X-Force to kill their leader Cable. Ultimately, Cable overwhelmed X-Force with his own immense but untrained telepathy, freeing them from their brainwashing.[41] Their alliance with Holocaust came to an abrupt end when they were attacked, and Tessa was effortlessly defeated by Onslaught, who had come to entreat Holocaust to capture Nate Grey.[42]

Shaw and Selene gathered together again, having Tessa to recruit the teleporting time-traveller Trevor Fitzroy to form a new inner circle for the Hellfire Club in order to combat the threat of Bastion's anti-mutant Operation: Zero Tolerance.[43] Selene presented Shaw and Tessa with a revived Madelyne Pryor. Tessa immediately recognized her and prompted the dismissive Sebastian to reconsider her membership.[44] Tessa sought to unravel the mystery of Pryor's revival by looking into her mind during her sleep, but Pryor woke up and defeated Tessa in a psychic duel. Initially considering to slay Tessa, Pryor just seemingly erased her memory of the event as a way not to raise suspicions from Shaw. Tessa's intrusion restored Pryor's memories, giving her the impetus to start her own schemes.[45] Tessa stayed watching from the shadows as Pryor used her wiles to secure for herself Shaw's backing at Selene's expense.[46] Ultimately, Tessa expressed her concern about the alliance between Shaw and Pryor, and was approached by Selene to jeopardize Pryor's influence.[47] However, Pryor and Shaw eventually lost interest in the new Inner Circle.

Still assessing data concerning artificial intelligence for Shaw's best interests, Tessa came upon knowledge about the reactivation of the robot X-51. As she sent Hellfire Knights to collect him, they failed in their mission, and Shaw ordered her to insist on gathering the specimen.[48] Tessa hired Mystique's Brotherhood to perform the mission, but, since X-51 had been rebuilt with anti-mutant Sentinel technology, they failed.[49] X-51's update got rid of Shaw's previous restrictions to prevent him from being recognized by Sentinel technology as a mutant,[50] making his destruction a priority for Shaw and Tessa. Tessa compiled all information about X-51's existence to provide Shaw with information that could undermine the robot's activities.[51] She contacted Shaw's ally Otomo to attack X-51 and passed the data she had collected to him, but Otomo was also defeated.[52] Eventually, with the intervention of the X-Men, X-51 got rid of his Sentinel programming, and Shaw and Tessa abandoned the ordeal.[53]

When Irene Merryweather investigated the origins and intentions of the Hellfire Club, she was kidnapped by Tessa. Under Shaw's suggestion, Tessa shared with Merryweather details about Shaw's ascension to the rank of the Black King of the Inner Circle. As the truth unfolded, Tessa and Shaw managed to persuade Merryweather not to publish the secrets behind the Hellfire Club.[54]

X-Treme X-Men[]

After years of patient waiting, Bogan exacted his revenge on Tessa, by capturing her, torturing her and branding her face with scars in the form of tears. Shaw chose not to save Tessa, for it could cost him to get into conflict against Bogan. Ironically, Tessa was rescued by an adversary, the leader of the X-Men Storm.[12] At Storm's side, Tessa affiliated herself with the X-Men, acting as part of their support crew and having completely parted ways with Shaw. Based at the Xavier Institute, she provided the X-Men with analysis and assistance during their training and missions.[55][56][57] During this period, she briefly met with Senator Robert Kelly to discuss his plans of running for president. Motivated by the death of his wife, he intended to promote an anti-mutant platform, but Tessa alerted him that his attitude was no different from the extremism of the terrorist Magneto.[13]

Storm decided to collect Destiny's Diaries, a series of prophetic relates that inconsistently described the future of mutantkind, for not trusting Xavier's idealism with such knowledge. Having formed a splinter group of X-Treme X-Men, Storm had Tessa join them as Sage, the team's intel support. As Sage, the identity that had been forced upon her was finally behind her.[14] Sage's resentment for Xavier was a strong motivation for her to follow Storm.[24][58] In their first mission, the new team of X-Men was led to Valencia, Spain. There, they were ambushed by the military as their moves had been predicted by the enhanced warrior Vargas, who had had access to the diaries and wished to execute the X-Men. Sage managed to escape,[8] but the attack was tragic for her teammates: Psylocke was slain and Beast was mortally wounded by Vargas.[59] In order to save Beast's life, Sage analyzed his genome to jumpstart and advance in his mutation and heal him of his injuries.[24]

As Sage kept track of all X-Men, she learned that Gambit has been framed for the murder of Australian crime-lord Viceroy.[60] While the X-Men investigated, Sage found herself trapped in an illusion replicating her time as Shaw's assistant in the Hellfire Club. Being able to see through the ruse, Sage knocked out the illusionist, Regan Wyngarde, who was under Shaw's service. Shaw planned to get revenge on Sage for deserting him.[61] She was freed thanks to the efforts of Heather Cameron, an Australian mutant with the ability to manifest various superhuman powers depending on what the current situation called for as Lifeguard. In the end, Sage deployed Lifeguard's abilities against Lady Mastermind, forcing her into a catatonic state. Shaw was able to escape scot-free using his influence to evade justice.[21]

The X-Men investigated the powers of the Madripoor Set, a collection of gems that could open a portal between dimensions. The demonic Shaitan attacked the X-Men and kidnapped Gambit and Lifeguard parallel to his intentions of activating the Madripoor Set. Sage suggested to follow Shaitan by catalyzing age catalyzed the latent mutant powers of Lifeguard's brother, Davis Cameron, who after agreeing with her offer joined the X-Men as the teleporter Slipstream. Arriving in Madripoor with his powers, the X-Men were welcomed by the ruthless Viper, the prince of the pirate nation, as the island was invaded by alien troops.[62] An uneasy alliance happened between the X-Men and Viper, which ended up tragically with her betraying them and causing Storm to fall captive to the clutches of the aliens' leader, Khan.[63]

Sage hacked into Khan's citadel's systems to collect information and proposed several plans to coordinate the X-Men into freeing Storm, which involved infiltrating the citadel.[64] Parallel to this, the powerhouse Rogue asked her teammate Sage to help her stabilize her control over the manifestation of the alternative powersets she had had contact with over the years. Reluctantly, Sage complied, allowing Rogue to resist the forces in Madripoor. Meanwhile, inside Khan's citadel, Lifeguard attacked Sage for her recent actions that involved tampering with the genomes of her teammates. Sage concluded that Khan's plans involved hopping across realities to conquer worlds, placing over the X-Men the responsibility of stopping him and saving the planet.[65] In the rescue mission, Sage managed to break into Storm's quarters and liberate her. Together, the two women schemed for the collapse of Khan's sanctuary.[66]

By infiltrating the invader's data network, Sage was instrumental in enacting the plan, but was brutally assaulted by the overlord after being caught by surprise. Ultimately, she was saved from death by Lifeguard. As Storm dueled Khan, she got lost in the labyrinthic base. The wounded Sage refused to leave the site as the other X-Men searched for Storm. In order to subdue her and bring her to safety, her teammates Bishop and Thunderbird knocked her out, expressing her humane preoccupation towards Storm.[67] After reemerging in Madripoor, the X-Men recovered from the attack. After the invasion was defeated, Viper reappeared, leading Sage to humiliate her and threatened her life if she ever crossed the X-Men again.[68]

Prisoner of Fire[]

As the X-Men restructured their roster and mission, leaving behind Destiny's Diaries and focusing on protecting humanity,[69] Sage expressed her worry about Xavier's immoral influence by refusing to return to his institute. In this setting, she forged a friendship bond with her teammate, Bishop. While investigating a murder case involving Jeffrey Garrett, a young mutant teleporter, in a glacier in Alaska. When in contact with the horrifying evidence, Sage recognized Elias Bogan was involved in the case since he owned the cabin where the crime had taken place and she was familiar with his modus operandi. Having been offered asylum at the Xavier Institute, Garrett was under the protection of Emma Frost. Sage and Bishop attempted to enter the school to approach Garrett and interrogate him and bring him to justice, only to be psychically fended off by Frost and her psychic students. Ultimately, Garrett cast Sage and Bishop away.[70] The case went to the public and caused an ideological schism concerning mutant-human affairs. Sage and Bishop worked with police investigators for a solution, while Storm contacted Xavier and Frost herself. Sage was suspicious, though, and probed the situation by herself, learning that Bogan had telepathically taken hold of Frost through Garrett.[71]

Heavily armed, Sage broke into the Xavier Institute alongside Bishop to put Frost down. However, they were trapped by Garrett inside the illusory environment of the Danger Room.[72] In the fabricated setting, Bogan replicated the crime scene while trying to torture Sage. He expressed his intentions of getting revenge against her and Frost for their past schemes against him. It was revealed Bogan's associates were sadists who molested Garrett and his family. Storm and Frost joined forces to stop the situation, allowing Sage to deactivate the Danger Room and put an end to the turmoil, forcing Bogan to vanish. In the aftermath, the two factions of X-Men, led by Storm and Xavier, shared their different stances on solving the case. Xavier attempted to reason with Sage by himself, but was met with coldness as she declared she remembered his good intentions and immoral means very well.[58]

Despite not having departed in good terms with Xavier, Sage returned to the Institute alongside Bishop in order to investigate the apparent murder of Frost. While interrogating suspects, Sage upgraded her systems to identify Hypercortisone-D, a drug that enhanced mutant powers and was being widely used at the Xavier Institute and being related to other incidents as well. She was led to the shack in the woods of the school, where she found a brood of insect-like eggs, but before she could act she got knocked out by an armed assailant.[73] Several bytes of Sage's recent memory were erased in the assault as she was found and rebooted by Bishop. In the shack, they found the weapon that fired the bullet that shattered Frost's diamond body. Sage found out the culprit to be Angel, who had laid the eggs in the shack. However, it was also obvious she had been psychically forced to do so by Frost's former pupil, Esme. When confronting Esme about her crime, Sage and Bishop were telepathically subdued as she escaped. Xavier freed them and, with his intervention, Sage recollected that in the shack she had been attacked not by Angel, but by a tall man, who happened to be Xorn, one of the teachers.[74]

In a following mission, the X-Men had been framed for bringing down an airship transporting the anti-mutant personality, Reverend William Stryker. When investigating the crash site, Sage was kidnapped by Stryker's Purifiers.[75] The X-Men overcame their attack, and Sage interrogated the defeated Purifiers, learning about a mutant sanctuary Stryker targeted named Mount Haven.[76] In Mount Haven, Sage experienced a feeling of bliss she had not felt in years by seeing young mutants being able to preserve their innocence. Bishop found this manifestation suspicious, since the stoic Sage would not reveal her true emotions in that fashion, even though they were genuine. As such it was revealed Sage was being controlled nanites deployed by the mutant supremacist intelligence that ruled Mount Haven, Reverend Paul.[77] When Reverend Paul was intercepted, Sage collapsed due to the deactivation of the nanites. Afterwards, Stryker was imprisoned in Mount Haven to prevent the nanites from being released onto the world.[78]

Sage and her teammates visited a now depowered Rogue in Valle Soleada, a mutant-friendly neighborhood in Los Angeles that was heavily influenced by the actions of X-Corp, a global organization founded by Xavier. The region was the location of several odd conflicts between mutants and humans.[79] Sage linked the events to those affected by it being victims of nightmares, including Marie D'Ancanto, a young anti-mutant terrorist who had her family murdered by the mutant vandals known as the Spikes. The secret influence behind the crisis was Elias Bogan, who wished to trap Sage.[80] Sage had her mind tampered with by Revenant, a servant of Bogan, but Rogue defeated the villain and followed them to the headquarters of X-Corp.[81] The X-Men retreated to make their move against X-Corp.[82]

When investigating the X-Corp headquarters, Sage learned it once belonged to the Hellfire Club.[83] She and Bishop orchestrated his own capture as a way to gain access to Bogan, sparking Rogue's ire for their reckless plan. This caused the X-Men to pressure Sage to reveal her past history with Bogan, which she did.[12] Sage managed to lead some of the X-Men into Bogan's cryptic catacombs that existed underground, underneath Los Angeles. Secretly, she planned to use the powers of Magma to obliterate Bogan and his base, but he was able to identify her plan. Mind-controlling Magma himself, Bogan engulfed Sage in lava.[84] He exposed her before the X-Men by sharing the massacre she had carried out when she met Xavier in the Hindu Kush. The X-Men did not rejected Sage as Bogan had predicted, instead they offered her acceptance, and freeing her from the molten rock prison. Together, the X-Men were able to overpower Bogan, with Rogue convincing Sage to ultimately not claim his life.[9] Despite having been defeated, Bogan's essence still lingered around, prompting Sage to stop him for good. In the catacombs, Sage and Gambit were not affected by his mental trap. The X-Men resisted his influence and forced him to disappear. Sage was finally free from her aggressor.[85]

Bogan was defeated and the X-Men enjoyed relative peace. In this situation, under Rogue's request, Sage surprised Gambit by reactivating his dormant mutant powers without his awareness.[86] Soon after, Storm led the X-Men to Chicago to help Kitty Pryde deal with the assassin ninja master Ogun.[87] Sage monitored their activities. Parallel to this, the anti-mutant organization Purity also made their move. One of the assassins for hire from the Galérer, Beasty-Brute, brutally attacked Sage.[88] As Ogun possessed Beasty-Brute, Purity was able to deactivate the mutant abilities in the city,[89] that was thrown into chaos. Counting with heavy weaponry, Sage helped with easing up tensions alongside the local authorities.[90] However, she could not use her analytical abilities to get to the best conclusion. She decided to converse with the angry mob that opposed to mutants by herself. After being hit with a stone, she shared the gospel of Charles Xavier about coexistence between humans and mutants. Thanks to her intervention, the conflict was de-escalated.[91]

Chasing Hellfire[]

Parallel to her activities with other X-Men, Sage also performed other tasks of her interest. She performed a heist by invading the office of Voldemir Zhivago, a former shareholder of the Hellfire Club who had access to personal files of her life prior to her association with Shaw. She was ambushed by Chung Yu, a formidable martial artist who acted as Zhivago's bodyguard. Being able to download content about her opponent's sensei, Sage was able to best him in combat and escape scot-free with her prize, pictures she held dear of her comrades in the periods of war. Despite being able to recollect the picture from her computer-like memory, Sage expressed the value the physical connection to things and people could be.[92]

Wishing to officially police mutant-related affairs, Storm sanctioned her X-Men team as a unit sponsored by the United Nations known as the X-Treme Sanctions Executive, or X.S.E..[86] As such, the team counted with full authority on mutant situations around the globe, being based at the Carriage House in the woods of the Xavier Institute, home of the other X-Men. Xavier was no longer headmaster of the Institute, having relinquished his position to Frost and Cyclops, which made Sage's return more comfortable. Sage operated a computer system that monitored the team's activities and analyzed relevant data for their operations. In her tower in the Carriage House, Sage counted with immediate access to information from multiple privileged databases in order to assist X.S.E.[93]

During one of their missions, Sage instructed the team to contact Brian Braddock, the otherworldly champion Captain Britain, at Braddock Manor. There, the team was easily subdued by the cybiote hero-killer known as the Fury.[94] Having access to the cybernetic sunglasses X.S.E. used for communications and intel, the Fury infected Sage's database with nanites and assimilated her. Taken over by the Fury, Sage proved a formidable threat to her fellow X-Men at the Xavier Institute until they were able to sever the connection established through the glasses.[95] Free from the mind control, the X-Men travelled to the Braddock Manor, where Sage acquired the Fury's core processing unit. She formulated a plan to combine the team's powersets to help Marvel Girl create a miniature black hole effect, which finally defeated the Fury.[96]

Before leaving the United Kingdom, the X.S.E. members were captured by Viper, who sought for revenge on the team, especially Sage, for the incidents in Madripoor. She trapped the group in one of Arcade's Murderworlds, where Sage was apparently executed by Viper herself.[97] It was soon revealed that Wolverine had used an image inducer to take Sage's place for they had been aware of Viper's plans prior to the kidnapping. This incident led Sage to learn that Sebastian Shaw was reforming the Hellfire Club's Inner Circle, since Viper was shown to have formed an association with Courtney Ross, the new White Queen. However, Sage kept the information a secret from her teammates.[98]

Sage ultimately accepted Shaw's invitation to return to the Hellfire Club and left the X-Men,[99] causing them to pursue her with Emma Frost's assistance.[100] Through Sage's machinations, Frost and Marvel Girl went to Ross and Viper in Hong Kong to dismantle a slaver operation in the Hellfire Club. Meanwhile, by having Bishop track Shaw, the other X-Men found Sage at the Hellfire Club Mansion. Sage had placed Roberto da Costa as the new Black King of the Hellfire Club. Before X.S.E. could decide on a position to take in these events, the obscene Donald Pierce intervened against them.[101] Pierce was defeated, but at the cost of Shaw's health. Sage then revealed that she had known the attack was likely all along, having predicted Shaw's incapacitation as a probable outcome. Her betrayal of Shaw led da Costa to ascend as the new Lord Imperial of the Hellfire Club. Sage permanently left the X.S.E. to advise and guide da Costa, believing she could ensure he was not to be corrupted by power, as Sebastian Shaw had been. However, her shady acts cost her the trust of her former teammates in the X-Men and in the X.S.E.[15]

Excalibur[]

During the celebration of Christmas, Sage reappeared in the X-Men Institute. She surprised Gambit to tamper with his genome, once again using his emotions as a catalyst to jump-start a regenerative process that cured his blindness.[102] Meanwhile, Courtney Ross and Viper discussed plans to take control of the devastated island of Genosha, formerly a mutant haven that was destroyed by Sentinels. They were cautious of Sage's influence in the Hellfire Club, with Ross ordering Viper not to send any electronic communications to evade Sage's attention.[103] This was followed by M-Day, an event cast by the Scarlet Witch that eradicated the majority of the mutant population. Sage was one of the few mutants that preserved their powers. She compiled all the information available to her and shared her impressions with Beast. Expanding on Emma Frost's initial findings, Sage concluded that not only mutants were depowered, but the very potential of humanity in generating additional mutants had also been erased.[104]

In England, while associated with Courtney Ross, Sage joined forces with Captain Britain, super-agent Pete Wisdom, and some of the X-Men against Shadow-X, a corrupted version of the original X-Men led by a sadistic Xavier.[16] Sage took up the position of coordinating the heroes to neutralize Xavier and rescue Nocturne. However, she was taken by surprise by the member of Shadow-X, Beast, being knocked out. Wisdom ultimately incapacitated Xavier, putting him under arrest. He proposed the reformation of the group Excalibur by recruiting the heroes who resisted Xavier, but found little support,[105] except for Sage, who stood by his side. Particularly worried about the effects of the mutant decimation, Sage observed how mutantkind and the X-Men adapted to their new condition and decided to help Wisdom in reviving Excalibur.[106]

Attacked by Warwolves, Sage was able to resist their attempt of possessing her mind. The attack was not isolated, since the other heroes Wisdom wanted to be part of Excalibur were also targeted. Realizing a hidden agenda ran parallel to their activities in the difficult setting of the mutant decimation, Captain Britain inspired the heroes to reform Excalibur.[107] The team's main interest was Albion, a version of Captain Britain who posited a danger. As the members of Excalibur acted as heroes, Sage analyzed data about Albion and Xavier to gain information. She supervised the transfer of Xavier, during which he was able to telepathically subdue her.[108] However, being immune to his psychic interference, Sage was able to disrupt his influence. Wisdom rescued her from being physically harmed. In the end, Sage executed Xavier for his transgressions.[109] The brain-dead Xavier was revealed to be the Shadow King. In a failed attempt to torture Excalibur, he was opposed by Psylocke, as Sage monitored their interaction.[110]

Amidst these machinations, Excalibur embarked on other adventures. They magically traveled to the Middle Ages to help the Black Knight save Camelot from an attack by the Makluans, which Sage uncovered and was able to elaborate a plan to stop them. In the past with no electronic devices, Sage found the lack of access to vast amounts of data somehow liberating.[111] In Korea, she led Excalibur to recover their teammate, the Juggernaut, who faced issues related to the source of his powers, Cyttorak. During the mission, the group expressed unspoken tensions among them, something Sage did not take a part of.[112] When her teammate Nocturne fell victim to a stroke, Sage supervised her recovery.[113] Disturbed, Nocturne complained about her presence. In order to evaluate her limitations, Sage let Nocturne use her possession abilities to control her body. Piloting Sage's body, Nocturne had no access to her memories, but enjoyed her physical prowess. When the consequences of the stroke manifested in Sage's organism, she took control over herself again and interrupted the possession. Afterwards, alongside Excalibur, Sage provided additional assistance to Nocturne.[114]

A top priority to Sage and Excalibur was intercepting Albion.[115] In order to acquire intel on Albion and his plans of conquering Britain, Sage created a new cover identity of Diana Fox, echoing her time as Tessa, to infiltrate his operations.[116] After making the first contact, Sage was put under interrogation by Albion for days, until she earned the position of joining his Shadow-Captains, a band of depowered mutants who wished to bring revolution upon the United Kingdom. By wearing one of his uniforms that enabled flight and super-strength, Sage's body was re-conditioned, but her mind was preserved thanks to her mutant abilities and psychic training. She was able to gain his trust and quickly became one of his right hands. However, when Albion empowered a technology-disabling artifact to achieve his goals, Sage made her move against her master. Albion's ally, Lionheart, identified the upcoming treason and attacked Sage with her sword. Wounded and in contact with the relic, Sage had her mind corrupted, and only her manufactured personality was active.[5] The forged persona of Diana Fox fully replaced Sage's spirit and, as Britannia, she became Albion's most loyal and dedicated soldier.[4]

At his side, she protected her master from the attacks of Excalibur, who had joined forces with Shadow-X through Wisdom's former organization, Black Air, following the massive deactivation of technology in Britain. Cyclops decapitated by Britannia after trying to kill Albion.[117] Britannia became a rival of Lionheart in this position, as the two operated as Albion's top soldiers. In another conflict against Excalibur, Britannia eliminated another member of Shadow-X, Beast.[4] Captain Britain dueled Britannia himself in an attempt to restore her memories as Sage.[118] Recognizing Albion posited a threat to the world, Lionheart defected and joined Excalibur. She battled Britannia, who was overwhelmed, especially due to Excalibur joining the fight. The difficult fight allowed Sage to gain strength. Ultimately, Wisdom put Britannia out of commission, which brought Sage back to control. This was a significant tactical advantage, since Sage was able to trick Albion as he fought Captain Britain. Defeated, Albion watched his device be destroyed and the country returned to normal.[119]

Exiles[]

With the defeat of the Shadow Captains, a party was held in honor of Excalibur for their contribution to saving the country. Sage felt guilt for her actions as Britannia. The party was interrupted by Psylocke, who had joined the dimension-hopping Exiles team.[120] Excalibur and the Exiles were targeted by the assassin Rouge-Mort, being drawn into a crisis to save the multiverse from Mad Jim Jaspers, who had merged with the Fury. As Captain Britain was wounded, Psylocke teleported the heroes to the Crystal Palace, which served as the Exiles' headquarters.[121] From there, it was revealed the Starlight Citadel, center of power of the Captain Britain Corps, was being attacked by Jaspers. Sage and Psylocke concluded that Albion would be an important ally in fighting back, since Captain Britain was incapacitated, convincing Wisdom to release him from incarceration.[122] In the Starlight Citadel, the heroes learned Merlyn, the ruler of Otherworld, was responsible for the schemes that enabled Jaspers. Merlyn's daughter, Roma, was found mortally wounded by Sage.[123] In her final moments, Roma transferred all of her knowledge about the Multiverse into Sage's mind. The overwhelming experience fractured Sage's mind and destroyed her sense of sanity. Merlyn and Jaspers were defeated by Excalibur and the Exiles and, afterwards, by expressing her fear of someone on Earth ever gaining the precious knowledge she now bore, Sage joined Psylocke and the Exiles.[17]

The knowledge imparted to Sage by Roma caused her to suffer from hallucinations. She soon proved to be a problematic addition to the Exiles, since without grasp on reality she would usually mistake them for enemies in the Crystal Palace.[124] Another complication was the re-emergence of the Diana Fox persona, who attempted to take control of Sage again due to her fragilized state. As a result, Sage had to constantly keep her concentration in order not to expose herself and others to danger. She did not take part in the missions the Exiles carried out in divergent realities either, constantly staying at the Crystal Palace.[125]

Staying in the Crystal Palace did not maintained Sage out of trouble, though. Its systems malfunctioned and teleported the Exiles Morph and Cat to world modelled after fairy tales. Witnessing the event, Sage decided to follow them. In the fantastic realm, Sage manifested her blocked telepathic abilities, an indication of the instability of her psyche. This put an additional strain in her efforts to keep control over herself. With her psychic powers, she learned about the forbidden love between the human prince Domenic and the dragon Marysal.[126] To help Domenic, the Exiles battled the mercenary Venger before returning to their allies in the Crystal Palace.[127] As the team's telepath, Sage was part of a mission in a world of warring empires.[128] Still disturbed by Diana Fox, Sage manifested flight abilities in addition to her psychic powers.[129] She and Morph allied themselves with the Queen of France, protecting her from attacks from Atlantis, but were subdued by Purge, an agent of Atlantis who wished to reprogram Sage for his interests. The extreme stress caused Sage to fully release Diana Fox. The powerhouse Britannia broke free from her shackles and unleashed her strength upon Purge. After defeating him, Britannia became once again dormant, with Sage having no recollection of what had transpired as the Exiles left the world after preventing a world war.[130]

The malfunctioning of the Crystal Palace caught Sage's interest. Cat also shared the concern, since she had been affected by the effect. As such, Sage convinced Cat to leave the Crystal Palace and join the Exiles in another dimension so she could assess the problem herself.[131] However, Diana Fox reemerged, in another attempt to take control over Sage's spirit.[132] The two halves of the same woman found enemies in common in the form of the physical manifestations of Roma's knowledge in the Crystal Palace. It was then revealed that the cause of the instability in the multiversal hub was provoked by Merlyn, who hunted Sage for her connection to Roma as a way to reinstate himself as Guardian of the Omniverse through the Crystal Palace.[133] Merlyn was able to defeat Sage, but Diana Fox still stood against him.[134] As a hero, Diana Fox protected Sage's life and sacrificed her own freedom as a way to fend Merlyn off and protect all of creation from him.[135] As a result, Sage merged with the Crystal Palace, becoming one with the Exiles team's computer base to stabilize it. Her decision was made so Cat would not fall victim to the process. In this position, Sage guided the Exiles in several different missions across the dimensions,[23] including training Psylocke with holographic projections in preparation to end the threat of Slaymaster.[136] Long term exposure to the Crystal Palace was extremely hazardous to the Exiles. Like Sage, the rest of the team was absorbed into its structure, and all entered a state of suspended animation.[137]

Multiversal X-Men[]

With no detailed explanations, Sage was freed from stasis and emerged on an alternate reality populated by sentient robots ruled by Prime Minister Danger. Humans had been exterminated and subdued by the machines. In this setting, Ambassador Sage represented the reconciliation commission for the interests of organic life in a leadership position in S.W.O.R.D. Earth had almost no humans left, and Sage reached an agreement with Danger to monitor the planet for the interval of three years to provide assistance for the survivors before permanently leaving the human-free world to the robots. When the mutant child Kurt Waggoner fell into the machines' clutches, Sage discussed the terms for his release.[138] Waggoner was a member of a dimension-hopping group of X-Men, who invaded Earth to rescue him. Patrolling the situation, Sage immediately rescued one of the X-Men as her former ally from Excalibur, Dazzler. She joined forces with them and broke the terms of the treaty to collect Waggoner. In a space-faring robot ship stolen from S.W.O.R.D., the mutants escaped Danger's reach.[139]

With her diplomatic role no longer viable, Sage joined Dazzler's group of X-Men, whose mission consisted of hunting down "evil" versions of Charles Xavier. At Dazzler's request, Sage lent the team her spaceship and her capabilities as a telepath to track down the versions of Xavier. Ironically, the first reality they visited was their own homeworld, only to find out the Xavier that had mentored them had died.[140] When the sorcerer Lord Xavier possessed the team's own "Disembodied Xavier Head" and resurrected "Nazi Xavier", he then proceeded to feed a multidimensional rift that contributed to the Exterminators escaping their prison.[141]

As the team attempted to deal with this threat they were joined by teams from other realities, and Sage and Dazzler were reunited with some of their former colleagues, including Wolverine and Gambit. Sage was teleported back to her home reality, where she interfaced with the "black box" of the Dreaming Celestial, uncovering the Exterminators' lost history. In doing so, she also ascertained that the only way to defeat them was once again to imprison them within a single universe.[142] When this was accomplished, Sage was left in her home reality with her fellow X-Men.[18]

Age of X-Man[]

Several years of no-contact with her fellow X-Men ended when Jean Grey's worldwide call for all former X-Men to battle a Life Seed-altered X-Man. Sage combined her psychic offense with that of Jean, the Stepford Cuckoos, No-Girl, and Psylocke, together ending X-Man's possession of Legion. Despite this, X-Man released a final blast of energy that appeared to completely annihilate all of his opponents.[143]

Those caught in the blast were transported by X-Man to a reality he created through the power of a Life Seed and given new lives.[144] It's unrevealed if Sage was transported to the reality with the others. If she was, she would have been released by X-Man after realizing the flaws in his new world.[145]

Krakoa[]

Sage later went to live on the sovereign Mutant nation-state of Krakoa, created by Charles Xavier, Magneto, and Moira X, where she worked with Cypher in the center of the interface for the Krakoa's gateway system.[19] She was also a member of Krakoa's X-Force, which served as the nation's equivalent to the CIA. [20]

Sage would spend many months as X-Force's "girl in the chair", serving under its leader Beast and advising and assisting field operatives including Wolverine, Domino, Deadpool, Omega Red, and Kid Omega.[146] But, after a number of horrific acts committed by Beast in Krakoa's national interest came to light, Beast would be forced to leave Krakoa and go into hiding, with Sage replacing him as head of X-Force.[147]

Personality

The foundation of my life has always been control. The world around me may be chaos but I was always grounded, always in control-- of my life, of my destiny.

—Sage[source]

It is important to consider that Sage lost her home at a young age, and only a few years later, was a lone wanderer in a war-zone hundreds of miles away. Exactly what traumas she experienced are unknown, but her experiences made her intensely self-reliant to the point that she rarely displays any weakness, nor does she readily ask others for assistance.[9]

Perhaps unsurprisingly, Sage does not value a sense of belonging over that of her cause, and will easily sacrifice her interpersonal relationships for her perception of the greater good.[148]

Sage wants to be in complete control of her situation at all times. To this end, she will engage in widespread surveillance while simultaneously employing secrecy even from those with which she works. Unfortunately, her allies are typically not soldiers, and as such, often resent being used as ignorant pawns, no matter how heroic the outcome or how justified the need for operational security.[84][148][105]

Attributes

Powers

Sage is a mutant whose brain functions are relatively analogous to those of a computer,[11][149][150] granting her the capacity to figure complex statistics from observable data for analysis, run multiple mental activities at once, perfectly recollect events from her memory, and the ability of editing her own systems.[151]

  • Mental Computation: Sage is a mutant with astonishingly advanced intellectual capabilities forged from a mind that works in the same way as a supercomputer would.
    • Photographic Memory: Sage perfectly remembers everything she experiences. She can instantaneously retrieve every memory and any information she has perceived.[92]
    • Multitasking: Sage is able to perform multiple tasks at once by allocating a partition of her brain to each task. For example, Sage can use her mind to replay a movie she had watched previously, play a game of internet chess, and focus on battling an opponent without any one task distracting her from another.[92]
    • Kinetic Memory: Sage can perfectly execute any skill she has had a single lesson in without the need for practice.[92]
  • DNA Perception: Sage can perceive the DNA in cells, living or dead, within close range. Combined with her innate analytical capabilities, this allows her to determine familial relationships between people[70] and to understand a person's current and potential abilities.[62][152]
    • Jump-start: After analyzing the DNA of another mutant, Sage is able to unleash latent mutant powers or enhance existing mutant powers. The process is irreversible and can often result in unpredictable side-effects.[24][62][153] She can also restore mutant abilities lost due to injury.[86]
  • Self-Cellular Manipulation: Sage has complete control over her personal physiology.[154] She once consciously used her immune system to purge herself of mind-controlling nanites.[95]
    • Limited Appearance Alteration: Her alter self, Diane Fox, was able to change the color of her eyes and hair and adjust her anatomy to accommodate her own personality.[155]
  • Telepathy: Trained by Xavier himself, Sage learned a great degree of telepathic talents that included reading[47] and detecting minds, communicating telepathically with others, counteracting psychic invisibility,[140] and attacking others with mental blasts.[156]
    • Astral Projection[155]
    • Illusions[citation needed]
    • Telepathic Immunity: Sage is capable of creating her own unique psionic protection against outside mental influence. By erecting a psionic "firewall" Sage is able to effectively protect herself from direct mental attacks, probes and detection of the mental variety while creating a defensive force that reflects any offensive mental action back at her attacker.[24] The wall is effective in both directions, nearly eliminating her own psionic abilities while active.[12]


  • Mutant Intelligence
Former Powers

While she was merged with the Crystal Palace, she had a solid, holographic body that could teleport anywhere within the confines of the structure, and she remained able to leave the Crystal Palace to join the Exiles. When she walked the grounds of the recreational rooms of the Crystal Palace, all manner of flora and fauna appeared.[157]

She could create holographic opponents for her team to train against.[136]

The Crystal Palace augmented her intelligence to Super-Genius level.[158]
Latent Powers
  • Telekinetic Flight: While under the influence of her alternate personality, Sage could fly and carry another person aloft, while displaying a tell-tale psychic power signature.[129]
  • Superhuman Strength: Sage's alternate personality, Diana, demonstrated superhuman levels of strength, easily shattering iron manacles and knocking an opponent flying with a single punch.[155]

Abilities

Gifted Intelligence: Sage's mutant intelligence makes her one of the smartest people on the planet through raw computational power and perfect recollection. This allows her to be an expert in every field that is well-documented or calculable, such as combat and technology, but less so for others, such as sociology and psychology.
Examples include:

  • Expert Engineer: Sage has displayed remarkable technical aptitude for electronics and mechanics, though she typically improves on existing designs rather than innovating new devices.
    Examples include miniaturizing the advanced technology contained within her cybernetic sunglasses, familiarity with repairing components of the X-Men's Blackbird,[159] and creating a solid-light version of an image inducer[160] and an autonomous support artificial intelligence.[161]
  • Peerless Hacker: Sage has access to countless digital records, public and private. She claimed she had access to any data network on the planet.[83] Her more impressive feats include gaining full access to the surveillance systems of a multiversal alien invasion force[64] and retrieving information from the "black box" of a deceased Celestial.[142]
  • Hyperpolyglot: Sage is fluent in over 123 different languages.[162]
  • Master Martial Artist: Sage is skilled in the martial arts, and is a formidable hand-to-hand combatant[12] due to her having complete conscious control over her own body.[154]
  • Weapons Expert: Sage is also self-taught in a wide range of firearms, which she can use with considerable skill and accuracy.
  • Interrogator: Sage's cold demeanor and ability to detect lies through body language has proven so intimidating that thugs and soldiers give up information quickly.[163][93]
  • Pilot: Sage was seen to skillfully pilot the X-Men's Blackbird,[164] and a space-mech.[140]
  • Medical Expertise
    • Trauma Treatment: Sage treated Captain Britain after he was nearly killed by Rouge-Mort.[121]
    • Forensics Expert: Sage is able to instantly and expertly identify a type of wound by its blood spatter pattern, pinpoint a shooter's position from bullet holes and determine causes of death of corpses.[165][70]
    • Mutant Genetics Expert: Sage is able to identify all kinds of genetic material, human, mutant and alien and, to a limited extent, simulate the effects of changing a genome.[24][166]


Indomitable Will: Sage was born with an indomitable will and was trained by Charles Xavier to enhance it further. This has allowed her to resist being controlled by nanites,[95][167] usurp possession,[114][135] and prevent telepaths from reading or influencing her mind.

Weaknesses

Alcoholism: Sage is an alcoholic, and uses drunkenness to give herself a break from the constant overstimulation of her computer-like brain. While drunk she is prone to fits of impulsiveness and rage, having destroyed some of her own research and physically assaulted Gorgeous George.[168]

Paraphernalia

Equipment

Cybernetic Sunglasses: Sage wears a pair of custom-built cyber shades that function as a wireless broadband computer network among like models.[10] The shades provide an audio/video feed via an in-built computer system outfitted with miniaturized conventional microphones and cameras, and can also project a three-dimensional image into the visual field of the wearer that can be expanded to allow others to see with whom the wearer is communicating or information they are reviewing.[12] The shades provide input by means of an augmented reality iconographic keyboard, and have an alternate visual input system for when a user's hands are otherwise occupied. The shades can be interfaced with any external data network whether terrestrial or alien in origin.[64] The shades also possess various sensors for detecting telepathic resonance patterns,[71] tracking energy trails,[70] and analyzing electronic circuitry, among others.

Weapons

Sage wields knives and a pair of handguns. She had a scoped rifle capable of holding stun ammunition, but she has yet to be seen firing it. Her accuracy was so precise, and her knowledge of anatomy so complete, that the X-Men did not object to her carrying guns, with the mutual understanding that she would not kill anyone while working with them.

Notes

  • The characterization of Sage was gradually implemented over the years. Creator Chris Claremont was responsible for most of the character's definition across the years:
    • She was originally introduced as Sebastian Shaw's servant, with no speaking role or mention to her name, in Uncanny X-Men #132 (January, 1980).
    • Years later, she was referred to as Tessa for the first time in Marvel Graphic Novel: The New Mutants (September, 1982), when her position as an employee of Shaw and the Lords Cardinals of the Hellfire Club was made explicit.
    • In subsequent appearances, Tessa was shown to be an exceptionally witty and intelligent assistant to Shaw, but with no objective mentions of her competence being a reflection of her being mutant. Her first line of dialogue that corresponded to her superhuman abilities as an analyst was uttered in Uncanny X-Men #182 (March, 1984), which characterized her following interactions with other characters in the years to come.
    • In the Hellfire Club entry of the Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe (Vol. 2) #5 (December, 1985), Tessa's photographic memory is mentioned.
    • In Classic X-Men #7 (January, 1997), Tessa is part of the group of mutant associates Shaw had gathered to gain influence in the Hellfire Club, which implied her status as a mutant.
    • Her status as a mutant was fully made explicit in X-Force #49 (October, 1995), which was not written by Claremont, but by Jeph Loeb. In this story, Tessa was classified as a regular telepath.
    • With Claremont's return to the line of X-Men books, Tessa was first introduced in his stories in X-Men (Vol. 2) #102 (May, 2000). In the X-Treme X-Men series (starting in May, 2001), Sage's background and powerset were considerably made more robust in definition.
  • Sage's powerset has been inconsistent across her publication history. Initially, her analytical abilities and exceptional memory comprised her abilities, with this characterization being provided by creator Chris Claremont.[151][11][149] In following appearances, she was portrayed as a low-level and ordinary telepath, with no specific mentions to her computer-like brain.[40][45] Following Claremont's return to the X-Men books, Sage's analytical powers were more explicitly described.[13][2][8] This could be seen as a misinterpretation of subsequent writers in comprehending the original nature of her powers. No mentions of her telepathic powers were made until X-Treme X-Men #41, when she mentions she had shunted off her psychic powers on purpose to protect her mind from the predator Elias Bogan. It is hinted in New Exiles #5 that Charles Xavier had been responsible for shunting off her telepathy by her own request. After years of not regularly appearing in stories, Sage returned in Uncanny X-Men (Vol. 5) #10, where she was placed alongside other telepaths. In X-Force (Vol. 7) #9, it was revealed Sage willingly deactivated her telepathy, which she had considered to be a distraction.
  • The tattoo-like scars on her face have disappeared at various times. A letters page — written by Jordan D. White[169] — with the conceit that the letters were being answered by Sage herself, wrote these off as her wearing makeup to conceal them.[134]
  • At an unspecified time in her life, Sage was sold to a harem, but escaped.[154]
  • Sage has a pressure point on her body that allows her to reboot her mind like a computer.[73]
  • While her rogue, manufactured personality, Diana Fox, was attempting to take over Sage's mind, Sage lost her detached emotional state,[170] lost control of her telepathy,[171] demonstrated self-propelled flight,[129] and, when Diana briefly took control, her hair and eyes changed color and she demonstrated a feat of super-strength.[155] This incident could suggest Sage's underlying powers are the combined telepathy/telekinesis suite seen in many other mutants, but maintaining her computer-mind state reduces the potential of her telekinesis to sensing and manipulating microscopic molecules.
  • There is often a misconception that Sage is able to control computers with her mind (cyberpathy/technopathy), or that she is a cyborg. Neither is the case. Sage must interact with computers in a normal fashion, or through her augmented-reality glasses. Though she possesses computer or machine-like attributes, these abilities are all functions of her mutant powers.[10][21] The term "cyberpathy" was used to refer to the mental processing abilities and advanced control over her bodily functions rather than the ability to mentally control computers.[83] Sage is fully human (mutant) without any cybernetic enhancements.[95]
