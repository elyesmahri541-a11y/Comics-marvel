Personal History

Gerard F. "Gerry" Conway was born September 10, 1952 in Brooklyn, New York City.[citation needed] Conway grew up in Queens, New York.[1] At age 13, a letter from Conway to Marvel was printed in Fantastic Four #50.[2] Conway was married to Karen Conway, and was the father of two daughters, Cara and Rachel.[3] Conway died of cancer in April 2026.[4]

Professional History

Gerry Conway began meeting fellow comic book fans who would go on to be his coworkers at Marvel at age 15 when he began taking weekly tours of the DC Comics offices in the summer of 1967. Fellow regular tour members included Marv Wolfman, Len Wein, Mark Hanerfeld, and Steve Mitchell. Through connections formed with DC staff members made during these tours, Conway began getting paid to primarily horror stories for both Marvel and DC at the age of 16.[5] [1] While the majority of Conway's anthology horror stories were sold to DC, a few were sold to Marvel including one that appeared in Chamber of Darkness #3 and another in Tower of Shadows #5.[6]

Through connections he had made in the industry, at age 17 Conway was contacted by Marvel editor Roy Thomas to write super hero stories directly for Marvel. His first assignment was a Ka-Zar story for Astonishing Tales #3, and he was soon after brought from DC to Marvel full-time and given ongoing scripting responsibilities for what were considered "second-tier" superheroes at the time, including Daredevil, Iron Man, and Sub-Mariner. By 1970, at age 18, Conway was given his initial "first-tier" hero to write for, becoming the ongoing writer for Thor.[1]

At the age of 19, Conway became the first writer entrusted with consistently scripting Spider-Man other than Stan Lee.[1] Conway was given the reigns to the new Spider-Man book Marvel Team-Up and four issues later was given charge over both current Spider-Man titles simultaneously: Team-Up and Marvel's flagship Amazing Spider-Man. His run, from issues #111–149 (August 1972 – October 1975), included the landmark death of Gwen Stacy story in #121 (June 1973).

Eight issues later, Conway and artist Andru introduced the Punisher as a conflicted antagonist for Spider-Man, as well as the Jackal. The Punisher went on to become a popular star of numerous comic books and has been adapted into multiple movies. Conway additionally wrote "Fantastic Four", from #133–152 (April 1973 – Nov. 1974).

Conway was occasionally roomates with Len Wein in Queens, New York, and in the 1970s began passing off writing responsibilities for Spider-Man to Wein, first through the Marvel Team-Up series then followed by Amazing Spider-Man.[7]

In 1976, Gerry Conway was replaced with Archie Goodwin as Editor-in-Chief of Marvel Comics.
