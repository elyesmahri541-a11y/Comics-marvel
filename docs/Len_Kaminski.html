Professional History

Len Kaminski was a writer and assistant editor for Marvel Comics. His first written works were in Peter Parker, The Spectacular Spider-Man #114 and Transformers #16 (May, 1986).

He wrote a brief run in Web of Spider-Man #22–26 before moving to a Werewolf by Night feature in Marvel Comics Presents #54–59. After writing for the Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe Master Edition, Kaminski started a substantial run in Iron Man #279–318, which overlapped with his work in Ghost Rider 2099 and co-writing duties in War Machine. In his Iron Man run, Kaminski created several now-trademark elements to the character's world, including the War Machine, Modular and Hulkbuster Armors. The introduction of the former of the three suits led to Jim Rhodes striking out on his own and adopting the alias of War Machine, an element that has become a staple of Iron Man's mythos. Kaminski was also the first writer to provide an in-depth exploration of Tony Stark's early life before becoming Iron Man, fleshing out both Howard and Maria Stark. Some of the other elements Kaminski introduced to Iron Man's world were the concept of the Iron Legion, notably adapted in Iron Man 3, and H.O.M.E.R., an AI assistant and the precursor of Iron Mans's later digital companions like J.A.R.V.I.S. or Friday. Kaminski's biggest collaborators during this era were artists Kevin Hopgood and Tom Morgan.

Kaminski left Iron Man ahead of the crossover event The Crossing, which saw the Armored Avenger revealed to be a sleeper agent working for Kang the Conqueror all along before having him killed off and replaced by a time-displaced younger version of himself. Unable to deter his editors from pursuing the story, Kaminski walked away because he didn't want to be "remembered as 'The Guy Who Made Iron Man Suck.'"[1] Kaminski's later Marvel work included Venom: The Hunger and Avataars: Covenant of the Shield, as well as Rune (Vol. 2) for Malibu Comics.

Kaminski often pastiched and referenced the Cthulhu Mythos, introducing new gods and eldritch entities (most of them later confirmed to be Great Old Ones, or Lesser Old Ones), such as Lloigoroth, Niggurab, Shigguraub, Yog-Sokot, and Azotharoth (the later confirmed to be the Outer God Azoth, that had appeared previously), the Trinity of Ashes, and the Innsmouth Hills Sanitarium.
