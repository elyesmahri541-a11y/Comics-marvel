Personal History

Moore was born in Northampton, England to Sylvia and Ernest Moore. As a child he lived in a lower class neighborhood called the Burroughs although he was often staying with his grandmother, who had no electricity or indoor toilet.

Moore got himself expelled from school in 1970 for distributing LSD, later describing himself as "one of the world's most inept LSD dealers". Later he entered the world of comic books, first trying his hand at being a penciler and then trying out being a writer. Eventually Moore received widespread commercial success for his comic-writing, however he turned his back on mainstream comics to develop other projects.

Moore had two daughters, Amber and Leah with his first wife, Phyllis and for a time they shared their bed with another woman named Deborah. Leah would later become a comic book writer also and marry John Reppion. Much later he married artist Melinda Gebbe.

He is a Neopagan and Occultist whose work has been embraced by numerous counterculture groups.

Professional History

Alan Moore worked with both of 'The Big Two' and co-created the only graphic novel on Time's 2005 "All-Time 100 Greatest Novels" list, namely "Watchmen". Away from Marvel and DC, he developed "The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen", one of numerous properties started by him of which he disassociated himself from the movie version.

Accolades

  • Alan Moore won Eisner Award in 1988 in "Best Writer" category for Watchmen (DC). For it he also won in "Best Writer/Artist" category. Watchmen (DC) by Alan Moore and Dave Gibbons won in "Best Limited Series" category. And also won in "Best Graphic Album" category.[1]
  • Alan Moore won Eisner Award in 1989 in "Best Writer" category for Batman: The Killing Joke (DC). It also won in "Best Graphic Album" category. And earned a nomination in "Best Single Issue/One-Shot" category. V for Vendetta (DC) by Alan Moore and David Lloyd earned a nomination in "Best New Series" category.[2]
  • Alan Moore earned a nomination for Eisner Award in 1991 in "Best Writer" category for Miracleman (Eclipse). It also earned a nomination in "Best Continuing Series" category.[3]
  • Alan Moore earned a nomination for Eisner Award in 1992 in "Best Writer" category for From Hell (Spiderbaby Grafix/Tundra) and Lost Girls (Spiderbaby Grafix/Tundra).[4]
  • From Hell (Spiderbaby Graphix / Tundra Publishing) by Alan Moore and Eddie Campbell won Eisner Award in 1993 in "Best Serialized Story" category. "The Nemesis of Neglect" chapter of From Hell earned a nomination in "Best Short Story" category. Alan Moore earned a nomination in "Best Writer" category for From Hell and Lost Girls (Spiderbaby Grafix/Tundra). Alan Moore and Eddie Campbell earned a nomination in "Best Writer/Artist" category for From Hell.[5]
  • A Small Killing (VG Graphics/Dark Horse) by Alan Moore and Oscar Zarate won Eisner Award in 1994 in "Best Graphic Album—New" category. Complete Bojeffries Saga (Kitchen Sink) by Alan Moore and Steve Parkhouse earned a nomination in "Best Graphic Album—Reprint" category. It also earned a nomination in "Best Humor Publication" category. Alan Moore earned a nomination in "Best Writer" category for 1963 (Image), Spawn #8 (Image), and A Small Killing (VG Graphics/Dark Horse).[6]
  • Alan Moore won Eisner Award in 1995 in "Best Writer" category for From Hell (Kitchen Sink).[7]
  • Alan Moore won Eisner Award in 1996 in "Best Writer" category for From Hell (Kitchen Sink).[8]
  • Alan Moore won Eisner Award in 1997 in "Best Writer" category for From Hell (Kitchen Sink) and Supreme (Maximum Press).[9]
  • "The New European" in Vampirella/Dracula: The Centennial (Harris) by Alan Moore, Gary Frank and Cam Smith earned a nomination for Eisner Award in 1998 in "Best Short Story" category.[10]
  • From Hell: Dance of the Gull Catchers (Kitchen Sink) by Alan Moore and Eddie Campbell earned a nomination for Eisner Award in 1999 in "Best Single Issue/One-Shot" category.[11]
  • Alan Moore won Eisner Award in 2000 in "Best Writer" category for League of Extraordinary Gentlemen (ABC), Promethea (ABC), Tom Strong (ABC), Tomorrow Stories (ABC), and Top Ten (ABC). Tom Strong #1 (ABC) by Alan Moore, Chris Sprouse, and Al Gordon won in "Best Single Issue/One-Shot" category. Tom Strong #4-7 (ABC) by Alan Moore, Chris Sprouse, Al Gordon, and others won in "Best Serialized Story" category. Tomorrow Stories (ABC) by Alan Moore, Rick Vietch, Kevin Nowlan, Melinda Gebbie, and Jim Baikie won in "Best Anthology" category. From Hell (Eddie Campbell Comics) by Alan Moore and Eddie Campbell won in "Best Graphic Album—Reprint" category. Top Ten (ABC) by Alan Moore, Gene Ha and Zander Cannon won in "Best New Series" category. The same series earned a nomination in "Best Continuing Series" category. "How Things Work Out" from Greyshirt in Tomorrow Stories #2 (ABC) by Alan Moore and Rick Vietch earned a nomination in "Best Short Story" category. "The Unbearableness of Being Light" from Jack B. Quick in Tomorrow Stories #2 (ABC) by Alan Moore and Kevin Nowlan earned a nomination in "Best Short Story" category. Promethea (ABC) by Alan Moore, J.H. Williams III, and Mick Gray earned a nomination in "Best New Series" category. The same series earned a nomination in "Best Continuing Series" category. Promethea #3 (ABC) by Alan Moore, J.H. Williams III, and Mick Gray earned a nomination in "Best Single Issue/One-Shot" category. Tomorrow Stories (ABC) by Alan Moore, Rick Vietch, Kevin Nowlan, Melinda Gebbie, and Jim Baikie earned a nomination in "Best Humor Publication" category.[12]
  • Promethea #10 (ABC) by Alan Moore, J.H. Williams III, and Mick Gray won Eisner Award in 2001 in "Best Single Issue/One-Shot" category. Top Ten (ABC) by Alan Moore, Gene Ha and Zander Cannon won in "Best Continuing Series" category. Alan Moore won in "Best Writer" category for League of Extraordinary Gentlemen (ABC), Promethea (ABC), Tom Strong (ABC), Top Ten (ABC), and Tomorrow Stories (ABC). Promethea (ABC) by Alan Moore, J.H. Williams III and Mick Gray earned a nomination in "Best Continuing Series" category.[13]
  • League of Extraordinary Gentlemen Vol. 2 (ABC) by Alan Moore and Kevin O'Neill won Eisner Award in 2003 in "Best Limited Series" category.[14]
  • Alan Moore won Eisner Award in 2004 in "Best Writer" category for League of Extraordinary Gentlemen (ABC), Promethea (ABC), Smax (ABC), Tom Strong (ABC), and Tom Strong's Terrific Tales (ABC).[15]
  • Top Ten: The Forty-Niners (ABC) by Alan Moore and Gene Ha won Eisner Award in 2006 in "Best Graphic Album—New" category. Absolute Watchmen (DC) by Alan Moore and Dave Gibbons won in "Best Archival Collection/Project—Comic Books" category. Alan Moore won in "Best Writer" category for Promethea (ABC) and Top Ten: The Forty-Niners (ABC). Promethea #32 (ABC) by Alan Moore and J.H. Williams III earned a nomination in "Best Single Issue/One-Shot" category.[16]

Notes

Alan Moore is not given credit as the writer in the Miracleman series and, instead, the credit is given to "The Original Writer" (which is, indeed, Alan Moore). This is due to a legal agreement reached by the two parties.
