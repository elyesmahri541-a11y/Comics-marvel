History

After an earlier armor was damaged in battle with the Masters of Silence, Stark donned an armor with more weaponry, which he called the War Machine.[2] It was further modified for James Rhodes, who started using this armor alongside his new alias, War Machine.[3]

Rhodey lost this armor after a time-travel adventure,[4] and started using and alien armor in Its place.[5] the Model 11 was eventually found by Parnell Jacobs, whom with aid from Stuart Clarke sold the armor to Baintronics, with the armor being converted to Baintronics technology, Jacobs started using the armor in multiple clashes with Iron Man as a mercenary for Sunset Bain.[6]

Recently, Rhodes returned using the Model 11.[7]

Properties

It possesses various weapons: repulsors, unibeam (added for Rhodes), twin chain gun, laser blade, wrist-mounted flamethrower.

The shoulder pack can deploy Gatling guns, a box rocket launcher, a pulse cannon, and a plasma cannon.

Various munitions are available: depleted uranium, high explosive, concussion, thermite, tear gas, tracer, flare, smoke, anti-tank, anti-ship, flame, fire suppressant, radio repeater or sub-nuke. Several non-lethal options were later added into the suit's MK II: baton, sabot, and rubber slugs for the cannon, concussion pulses from the hands, and strobes via the unibeam.[8] The armor comes equipped with a magnet in its hand and state-of-the-art wound coagulant that is administered via needle from the gauntlet.[9]

This model has more plating and shielding than the typical Iron Man armor. A poly-carbonate coating further improves the surface. An additional poly-prismatic coating was temporarily used to counter the Living Laser's powers. It presumably retained the photon shield from previous armors. The armor could withstand a bomb made from a higher yield of the Wonder Man's energy at point-blank range.[9]

Trivia

  • According to the War Machine Armor's co-creator Len Kaminski, the closest thing to a direct inspiration for the War Machine Armor was the cover for Incredible Hulk #390.[13]
  • The War Machine Armor's designer Kevin Hopgood has described the process the following way: "The design brief was 'It's Iron Man— but cooler!' so I had a lot of leeway! The black-and-silver idea came quite quickly. It's basically a punk rock / heavy-metal version of Iron Man. I was thinking of the armor as a mobile weapons platform, so the shoulder-mounted weaponry was a logical extension of that concept. It was Iron Man as a walking tank."[14]
