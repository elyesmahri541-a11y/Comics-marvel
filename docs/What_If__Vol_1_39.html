Synopsis for "What If Thor of Asgard Had Met Conan the Barbarian?"

The story begins with the Watcher recounting a tale about a meeting between heroes of different ages that happened on one of the alternate Earths in the myriad realms of reality. He begins by recapping something that happened long ago on Earth-616 when Thor, after defeating a pair of Storm Giants in Jotunheim, followed his half-brother Loki into a cavern. Finding a large number of tunnels that branched off in many directions, Thor followed one that led him to another place and time, ancient Troy in the midst of the Trojan War. However, the Watcher's story is about what if Thor had chosen the tunnel just to the left of the one which led to Troy. At that critical moment, a new reality had come into being, diverging from Earth-616, as Thor wandered down a tunnel that led to ancient Hyboria.

In the past of Earth-83600, Thor arrived during the Hyborian Age, possessing his hammer Mjolnir but without his memory and lacking his full godly strength. Finding himself in unfamiliar hills and not even knowing who he was, Thor asked a passing squad of soldiers (who were hunting for a black-maned thief) where he was and was told that he was in Corinthia, which was far away from his presumed home among the fair-haired Aesir of the north. At that moment, Conan tried to push some rocks onto the soldiers, and Thor happened to get in the way. As the soldiers fled, Thor spotted the scoundrel above who had caused the avalanche and climbed up the hill to confront the black-haired barbarian. Although Conan stated that he had no conflict with an Aesir, Thor attacked him and the two began battling fiercely. At one point, Thor's hammer knocked the sword out of Conan‘s hand so Conan picked up a large boulder and threw it at Thor, knocking him down. They subsequently engaged in hand to hand combat, and Thor threw Conan further than he’d ever been thrown before. Impressed, Conan stated that Thor had won his respect and that he would no longer seek his foe's early death, prompting Thor to agree to end their battle. Conan then declared that Thor would be his companion and presented him with a sword taken from a dead guard, and the two of them set out to get some coins for themselves by robbing some local moneybags.

That night, after moonrise, Conan and "Strawhair" robbed a fat priest, prompting Thor to ask if it was right for them to rob, especially in a temple of the gods. Later, in a tavern, Thor asked about Conan's gods and Conan told him that Crom was a grim god who little cared if men lived or died, and warned that it’s better to stay silent than to call his attention to you. When Thor then asked where he should seek Crom's mountain, Conan instead told him about how he would like to track down the Stygian wizard Thoth-Amon whose sorcerous image had appeared before him the other day. However, Thor insisted that he wanted to search for Crom and Conan eventually agreed to accompany him.

Later, in Cimmeria in the dead of winter, the duo battled and killed some wild boars not long before they reached the base of Crom's mountain. They both began scaling it, with Thor growing stronger as he climbed, but Conan was unable to keep up and decided to stop and roast some boar's meat. After reaching the mountaintop, Thor encountered four robed figures who, when asked, pointed the way to the throne of Crom. Arriving at the throne, Thor tried to address Crom, who was seated on his throne and initially didn't appear to notice him, but then reacted with anger at being spoken to by an "usurper." Crom then revealed that his visitor was Thor of Asgard, a successor to a host of northern gods and an immortal of another, later age. Thor's presence in Hyboria was not welcome because it was the Hyborian gods' time of power, and Thor's strength was diminished because he was so far from Asgard, his realm of power. Once Thor revealed that he had regained his memory, Crom demanded that he yield his hammer as a token of his repudiation of power in that time. When Thor refused, Crom was able to summon it to his hand, and he then hurled it away, to be buried deep within the bowels of the earth, where Thor would never find it. Dismissed by Crom, Thor descended to rejoin Conan, who mentioned that it had been only moments since they parted, although for Thor it had been hours. Thor then told Conan who he truly was, and Conan retorted, "It seems I’ve underestimated your imagination as earlier I did your strength, friend."

In Stygia, after having made blood offerings to Set, Thoth-Amon watched in a crystal ball as his winged messenger was led to a certain spot in the Earth's crust from which it retrieved a mysterious, wondrous metal from another reality, a mystic hammer which he would use to become the master of all the sorcerers of the Black Ring in the City of Magicians. Although the hammer initially resisted Thoth-Amon's chanting, it eventually yielded its powers of the storm, and Thoth-Amon began using those powers to force the rulers of kingdom after kingdom to surrender their thrones to him or have their realms washed away by massive floods.

On a quest to find Thor's hammer, Thor and Conan traveled from city to city until, eventually, a shopkeeper in Stygia's main bazaar told them that Thoth-Amon had it in the City of Magicians. After travelling there, Thor and Conan overcame the guards outside Thoth-Amon's throne hall. Finding it empty, they descended to a temple hidden beneath the palace where they interrupted a human sacrifice. where Thoth-Amon was calling down lightning onto the altar with Thor’s hammer. While Conan battled some Snake Men to keep them occupied, Thor confronted Thoth-Amon and grabbed hold of Mjolnir even as the wizard attempted to use it to call down lightning to continue the sacrifice. After slaying all of the Serpent Folk, Conan turned to see Thor and Thoth-Amon struggling over the hammer, with Thor calling upon Mjolnir to not let the lightning strike the mortals upon Set's unholy altar. When Thor calls upon the lightning to strike him, its master, the lightning obeyed, causing Thor to fall to the floor. Then, protective of its master, the lightning assaulted Thoth-Amon, with its power shattering his body and dark soul, reducing him to ashes within an empty robe.

In the aftermath, Thor told Conan that he was dying because, in his weakened state, he could not resist the lightning arcs which had struck him. Thor then told Conan that benevolence was the true way of the gods, not Set's bloodthirst nor Crom's indifference. With his dying words, Thor charged Conan to take Mjolnir to Crom and tell him that Thor of Asgard would never cast Crom's pantheon to chaos because he had perished so that men might live, and to offer up to Crom the hammer as a symbol of the love that deities of a later age will share with reverent mankind. Although still not certain if Thor was a god or a madman, Conan did his bidding because the memory of their friendship meant much to him. Taking Mjolnir with him, Conan returned to Cimmeria and began to climb Crom's Grey Mound.

The Watcher ended his story there, stating that whether or not Conan ever reached the summit and stood before Crom did not matter because it was on that day that Conan the mortal took his first step toward becoming much more.

Notes

  • Although the issue number isn't mentioned in the story, the divergence point between this universe and Earth-616 is "Thunder Over Troy." On Earth-616, a young Thor went through the tunnels and ended up participating in the Trojan War. Here, Thor went to the tunnel on the left instead, and ended up in Ancient Hyboria instead.
    • Since the Watcher begins his story by recapping events that occurred on Earth-616, the Thor, Loki and Storm Giants who appear in the first three panels of page 2 are not from Earth-83600. Although the Earth-83600 version of Loki and those two Storm Giants undoubtedly exist, they were not depicted in the new material of this story.
  • Since this story involves time travel, it's unclear how many divergences actually take place. While the Watcher does state that a divergence occurred when Thor made a choice while within the cavern, that should only affect Earth-616, diverging another timeline in which Thor never returned from the past. In addition to that divergence, it was Thor's arrival in the Hyborian Age that should have caused Earth-83600 to diverge from Earth-616 at that point in the past.
  • Although not named in this story, the cavern/tunnels through which Thor traveled to Ancient Hyboria had been officially identified as the Cavern of Time in "A Gazetteer of Asgard and Environs" that was published in Thor Annual #10.
  • Conan's mention of having seen a vision of Thoth-Amon's demon-face "the other day" indicates that this story takes place soon after the end of Conan the Barbarian #7 since that was when the Earth-616 Conan saw that vision while in the city of Numalia in Nemedia. This would seem to imply that this story starts early in Conan the Barbarian #8 when, on Earth-616, Conan ambushed some Corinthian soldiers who were tracking him, as shown in this story. However, there are some small but significant differences between the two situations. In the Earth-616 version of events, Conan was being tracked by six soldiers who were carrying torches to light their way in the pre-dawn twilight when he started the avalanche that killed five of them, leaving the leader, Captain Burgun, alive to fight and seemingly be killed by Conan. However, in the Earth-86300 timeline, Conan was being tracked in daytime by the four soldiers to whom Thor spoke, and all of them were either dead or fled after the avalanche.
    • While this would seem to indicate that the two timelines actually diverged sometime before Thor's arrival in the past, when the search for the thief was being arranged, it could also mean that, prior to ambushing the six soldiers led by Burgun, the Earth-616 Conan had previously used a similar ambush on four other Corinthian soldiers.
