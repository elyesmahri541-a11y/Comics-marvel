Synopsis

Overview[]

Ragnarok is the eternal cycle of death and rebirth for the Asgardians. At the end of this cycle, Asgard is destroyed and the Asgardian gods die. Eventually, the gods will be reborn, and the cycle will begin anew. The cycle was perpetuated by the enigmatic beings known as Those Who Sit Above in Shadow, who fed off the energies released during Ragnarok for sustenance and power.[4] Whether they had created the cycle, taken advantage of it, or were themselves created by it, remains unknown.[5] With each cycle, they would grow more powerful, eventually being able to predict every action taken by the Asgardians down minute detail.[6] While their existence was spoken of as a legend among Asgardians, only Odin himself had met them in person in every cycle.[5] This cycle was shown to be part of the Celestial Axis of the Zodiacal Procession. Asgard and eight of the Nine Worlds within are linked to the planet Earth in another dimension via the celestial equator.[7]

A Ragnarok cycle happens roughly every 2160 years, though with a degree of variation.[7] Each new cycle would occur largely the same but with notable differences. The Asgardian Gods, with no memories of past cycles, would relive similar events over and over, sometimes being different in appearance,[7] sometimes different in overall character.[8][9] The familial relationships between fellow gods also varied from cycle to cycle.[7] The Enchantress known as Amora in the present cycle has been known by many names including Freyja, Iduna, Gefn, Mardöll, and Idun in the previous cycles.[8] Only Odin would have the knowledge of repeated events, despite himself perishing during Ragnarok.[10] These differences in past cycles explain the differences in Asgardian legends about their own past. The Icelandic sags recorded by Snorri Sturluson specifically describe events in the previous cycle, explaining their differences from the current Asgardian history.[10]

In recent cycles Odin, seeking the break the looped existence his people were trapped in, would send his son Thor to Earth to learn humility,[9] in hopes that it would make his actions less predictable to Those Who Sit Above in Shadow.[11] Although other Asgardians have no memories of the previous cycle, they are aware of the upcoming Ragnarok due to it being foretold by Volla the prophetess. According to her, Ragnarok will be preceded by two major events:

  1. First, a bitter, unrelenting winter known as the Fimbulwinter,[12] will blanket Asgard, causing widespread fear and paranoia. Driven to the point of madness by the oncoming Ragnarok, infighting will break out across the land, with friend attacking friend, and brother attacking brother. Secondly, Loki will trick the blind god Hoder into killing Balder with a mistletoe.[13]
  2. Heimdall will sound his Gjallarhorn, officially signaling the beginning of Ragnarok. An army of Trolls and Giants led by Loki will charge across the Bifrost on their way to Asgard. After destroying the rainbow bridge, Heimdall will be swallowed by the invading horde. Odin will lead a counterattack and a final, cataclysmic battle will begin.
  3. As Asgard crumbles around them, Thor and Loki will meet each other in combat for one final time, but their fight will be interrupted by the sudden appearance of the Midgard Serpent. Though Thor will succeed in slaying the Midgard Serpent, the whole realm of Asgard will be torn asunder, leaving it in ruins. From Muspelheim will rise Surtur, the fire demon, who will proceed to burn what remains of Asgard to cinders.

Known Cycles[]

No reliable dating for the Ragnarok cycles exist, nor is it known whether and how much time passes between the cycles. Odin Borson was already active around 1,000,000 B.C. and looking like a man in his prime,[14] but whether the Ragnarok cycle already existed that far back remains unclear. Asgardians have become widely known on Midgard during the Hyborian Age, being frequently invoked and inspiring names of various places and peoples, this being the earliest direct evidence of a previous cycle. A minimum of 25 cycles have occurred, the highest known number being the 23rd,[15] and at least two more following it since, the previous Ragnarok taking place not long before the birth of Jesus,[10] and latest Ragnarok occurring during the Modern Age.[16]

Era of the Third Ragnarok[]

During this Ragnarok cycle, Idun the Enchantress held the role of the collector of the Golden Apples of Yggdrasil, but she would become the current Amora and not the current Idunn. In this incarnation, Thor Odinson was a ruthless warrior who had little time or interest in the revelry of his fellow Asgardians who during this cycle lived opulent and hedonistic lifestyles between battles. The people of Asgard were not immortals, but enjoyed an extended life span and vitality from the Golden Apples that came from the World Tree Yggdrasil, which were picked by the Enchantress. While the other warriors gorged on these apples, Thor rationed him, choosing only to consume his supply before going into battle. In battle against an army of Frost Giants, won thanks primarily to Thor, a wall surrounding Asgard was broken that was so old, nobody remembered when it was build. After the battle, a Frost Giant posing as a mortal human offered to fix the gates of Asgard, promising to complete the task within six months. If he succeeded, he would be given the Enchantress as a bride. Odin accepted this bet thanks to coaxing from Loki. When the giant nearly succeeded, he was denied the prize and revealed itself. When he attempted to attack, Thor slew the giant.[8] During the Third Age of Odin, the Asgardians enslaved the Succubi, leaving a scar on their people.[17]

Era of the Eleventh Ragnarok[]

During the eleventh Ragnarok cycle, Loki tricked the Idun the Enchantress into going to the leader of the Frost Giants, which led to her being taken as a concubine. When the Asgardians realized this and that they were cut off from the power of the Golden Apples, they attempted to get more from Thor Odinson he refused them. Thor did not act until Loki attempted to save the Enchantress and was hunted by the ruler of the giants. Thor slew the Frost Giant, though he was none too impressed with how careless his fellow Asgardians had been.[8]

Era of the Twenty-Third Ragnarok[]

During the twenty-third Ragnarok cycle, the daughter of the Frost Giant slain by Thor Odinson over the life of the Enchantress came seeking reparations from Odin for slaughtering her daughter. Thor was present when Odin agreed to present three gifts to the female Frost Giant and sent Loki to Nidavellir to have them constructed by Dwarves known as Grerr, Alfrigg, and Dvalin. One of these items was a sword that could slay Frost Giants, and Odin used it to kill the Frost Giant in his court. The Dwarves then returned to Nidavellir with the other two items, a golden arm band and necklace. The Enchantress sought to claim the necklace for herself and had sex with the Dwarves in order to claim it for her own. When Odin discovered this, he angrily took the necklace from her. Furious at this affront, the Enchantress then put a curse raising the dead and unleashing them upon Midgard. Thor was sent to Midgard to defend Odin's followers. He traveled there on his chariot pulled by his eight horses. He then had his Midgardian worshippers make a number of sacrifices so that Thor could control the massive Blood Colossus, warning the humans not to eat his horses no matter how hungry they became. After Thor destroyed the army of the dead, he found that the people disobeyed his command and ate his horses. Thor angrily caused massive flooding to the land of his followers before returning to Asgard. There Thor used magic to create a new horse out his friends' remains, the mighty Sleipnir.[15]

Giving into his rage, Thor began waging war across the Nine Worlds of Asgard. Over the next 40 days and nights Thor shattered the roots of Yggdrasil, spat in the Well of Mimir, laid waste to Jotunheim, stricken Midgard with floods and famine, and waged war on both Svartalfheim and Hel. This so angered Odin that he commanded Brunnhilda and the Valkyrie to stop Thor's rampage. The thunder god was brought low as he battled in the realm of Niffleheim. However this attempt to apprehend Thor quickly ended when a massive Frost Giant attacked them and Thor helped vanquish the creature. Afterward, abandoning his previously dislike for revelry, he celebrated his victory with the Warriors Three and the Valkyrie much to the anger and irritation of Odin. Odin easily bested Thor in combat and then stripped him of his godly stature and send him to Earth in the mortal guise of Arkin Torsen, a village doctor with a lame leg who required a walking stick to move about. This, Odin hope, was to teach his son humility.[9]

Previous Ragnarok[]

An account of the previous Ragnarok that took place over 2000 years ago was was revealed to Thor by Odin's disembodied eye. This cycle's incarnation of Thor was unique compared to others in that he was depicted with red hair, as opposed to the more common blonde. He also had two adult children Magni and Modi, while Odin's brothers Vili, Ve were still around as well. This version of Ragnarok completed it cycle in much the same fashion as Norse myth, with the accidental death of Balder and Loki's invasion of Asgard. While defending his home from Loki's horde, Odin was killed and devoured by the Fenris Wolf. In response, Odin's son Vidar decapitated the beast with his sword. Meanwhile, Loki and Heimdall killed each other fighting atop the Bifrost. The Midgard Serpent attacked Thor, sinking his massive fangs into the thunder god's flesh. Thor managed to free himself and ultimately defeat Jormungand, but was poisoned by the Serpent's venom and died.

Surtur slew Frey before rising from Muspelheim to hurl fire across the realm. Nine Asgardians survived the fall of Asgard: a revived Balder, Odin's brothers Vili and Ve, Odin's sons Vidar and Vali, Hoenir, Hoder, and Thor's sons Magni and Modi. On the day Jesus Christ was born in the town of Bethlehem, these last survivors of the last Ragnarok cycle began to recreate Asgard for the next cycle.[7] They found that a new land had formed on the remains of Asgard and decided to rebuild their home there, though they soon felt the memories of it fading quickly. Vili, Ve, Vidar, and Vali discovered the discarded spear of Odin, Gungnir. The four gods grasped the spear together and were transformed into a composite entity, becoming the present Odin, the new All-Father of the next age of Gods, who proceeded to recreate Asgard and the fallen Asgardians.[10]

Final Ragnarok[]

When Odin sent Thor to Earth to learn a lesson in humility by becoming human, a loophole was created in the cycle, as Those Who Sit Above in Shadow could no longer predict what Thor would do when influenced by man, and thus could not keep Thor bound by his strand of fate. This was part of Odin's plan. When the final Ragnarok arrived, Thor hung himself on Yggdrasill to gain vast wisdom and runic magic, and learned who was behind the cycle. Thor destroyed the Loom of the Fates, and broke the thread that held the destiny of Asgardians, from which Those Who Sit Above in Shadow were absorbing energy. With their source of energy destroyed and the cycle broken, Those Who Sit Above recreated Asgardian worlds and people to be no longer bound by destiny.[6]

The Final Incursion[]

Those Who Sit Above in Shadow would gain the last laugh when the end of all things came anyway, not just for Asgard, or even the universe, but the for Multiverse itself. After the final Incursion destroyed all of reality, the trickster Loki was one of the few survivors, having used magic to both exit reality and safely contain the essence of his fellow Asgardians.[18] After finding himself in a blank void of nothingness, Loki was confronted by Those Who Sit Above in Shadows, who demanded to be given the Asgardians' essence to feed on it. Loki defied Those Who Sit Above in Shadow, and even put into question their origin and existence. Unable to answer or even counter the questions posed by Loki, Those Who Sit Above in Shadows departed.[19] The Multiverse was eventually restored, with the Eighth Cosmos being a direct continuation of the Seventh, with most inhabitants not remembering the multiversal collapse and not being aware of any changes.[20][21]
